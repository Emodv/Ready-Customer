Economics of qualified customer opportunities

Businesses compare the fee for a matched, consent-based request against the cost of retainers, ad spend that produces low-intent traffic, and sales time spent on unqualified inquiries. No sale is guaranteed.

The comparison most owners run

Service businesses in Canada often face two cost structures:

  • Monthly marketing retainers or ad budgets that generate website traffic, form fills or calls of mixed quality.
  • A per-opportunity fee for a request that has already been filtered for service fit, geography, timing, contactability and basic economic fit.

The second model shifts spend from volume acquisition toward match quality. The business still owns the conversation, quote and close.

Costs that sit outside the fee

  • Sales time on inquiries that never match the ICP.
  • Follow-up labour on contacts that never reply or that lack buying intent.
  • Opportunity cost of capacity used on low-probability work.

A qualified opportunity reduces those downstream costs when the filter matches the criteria the business set. It does not remove them entirely.

What the model does not claim

  • A fixed conversion rate.
  • A guaranteed return on the opportunity fee.
  • That every matched request will accept a quote.
  • That pricing or terms are standard across categories.

Participating businesses set their own ICP, minimum value and acceptance rules. Outcomes depend on response speed, quote quality, market conditions and the customer’s decision.

Pilot note

ReadyCustomer is currently focused on flooring in the GTA / Ontario with defined qualification thresholds. Other categories are stored but not automatically routed. Rules can be adjusted as real acceptance and outcome data accumulate.
